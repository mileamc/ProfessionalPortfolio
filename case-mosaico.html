Mosaico

A structured writing tool designed to support narrative development and creative exploration.

My role

Description

Mosaico is a structured digital guide that helps writers develop narratives through a step-by-step creative process.

Context

Creative writing involves coordinating multiple elements — characters, plot, chronology, setting, and narrative progression. The question was not simply whether users could follow the method, but whether a structured process could support creative writing without limiting individual ways of working.

Research

A longitudinal evaluation

Instead of evaluating Mosaico through a single usability session, I followed participants over 12 months. The research combined three methods:

  • Digital Logbook

    Ongoing documentation of participants’ experiences.

  • Semi-structured Interviews

    End-of-study conversations about understanding, challenges, and adaptations.

  • QUIS Questionnaire

    Measurement of satisfaction with the interaction experience.

Research setupThe three instruments together: a digital logbook screen, interview notes, and the QUIS questionnaire or its results.

Participants

8 participants

20–52 years old

The group included both beginners and participants with previous creative writing experience.

What I evaluated

I evaluated Mosaico across four dimensions:

  • Effectiveness

    Could participants progress through the process independently?

  • Learnability

    Could they understand the guide and its instructions?

  • Adaptability

    Could they adjust the structure to their own creative process?

  • Satisfaction

    Did the experience feel useful and appropriate for their needs?

Findings

What the data showed

The evaluation revealed a distinction between following the structured process and adapting it to individual workflows.

  • 5/ 8

    Completed at least 18 of 25 steps independently

    All participants completed the timeline activity, indicating that the core process was accessible to most participants.

  • 5/ 8

    Understood the tool after one reading

    Three participants needed to revisit parts of the material.

  • 7/ 8

    Recalled the names of the steps

    However, only four could explain the purpose of each step, highlighting a gap between recognizing the structure and understanding its underlying logic.

  • 2/ 8

    Adapted the process to their own workflow

    While most participants followed the proposed sequence, two modified the order or format of activities to meet their individual needs.

Beyond the numbers

The qualitative research helped explain how participants interacted with the structure.

The process supported progression

Participants described the steps as interconnected and progressive, providing a logical path through narrative development.

Flexibility emerged through individual adaptations

Although most participants followed the proposed process, two reorganized or modified activities. This demonstrated that adaptation was possible within the structure, rather than being a behavior shared across the entire group.

From research to product

The findings highlighted opportunities to improve the balance between structured guidance, comprehension, and flexibility.

  1. 01

    Improve onboarding and comprehension

    Participants could often recognize the steps without fully understanding their purpose. The introductory material and explanations needed to communicate the logic of the process more clearly.

  2. 02

    Support flexible workflows

    While most participants followed the proposed sequence, some adapted activities to their own needs. This reinforced the value of allowing flexibility within a structured method.

  3. 03

    Reconsider external validation

    Participant feedback questioned the role of external reader validation, informing changes to how reflection and validation were integrated into the process.

  4. 04

    Reduce perceived complexity

    The visual language and terminology could be refined to make the guide easier to approach and understand.

Design changes

01 — Simplified introduction

I rewrote the introductory content using clearer, more approachable language and reduced unnecessary technical terminology.

Before and afterThe original introduction beside the rewritten one, so the change in language is readable.

02 — Restructured validation

Research feedback led me to rethink the role of external validation within the process. I removed and reorganized validation steps, introducing Internal Validation as a stronger point of reflection within the writing process.

What changed

The research informed changes to make Mosaico’s structured process clearer, more approachable, and better suited to different ways of working.

Next steps

The research also identified opportunities for further product development.

  • Educational testing

    Evaluate Mosaico in teacher-facilitated classroom settings.

  • Digital experience

    Continue refining the interface and instructions.

  • Broader research

    Test the method with more specific audiences and writing contexts.

  • Usability validation

    Complement longitudinal research with moderated usability testing, Think-Aloud, and standardized usability measures.

Outcome

The evaluation showed that most participants could progress through Mosaico’s structured writing process, while a smaller number adapted activities to their individual workflows.

The research identified opportunities to improve onboarding, clarify the purpose of each step, and make the experience more flexible and approachable.

I translated these findings into changes to the introduction, validation structure, and visual language, using participant feedback to guide product improvements.

The study also highlighted opportunities to investigate how structured creative tools can support different learning needs and educational contexts.

Final showcaseThe three or four strongest screens or artefacts, composed together — the last thing a reader sees of the product.

Key takeaway

A structured learning experience can provide guidance while leaving room for individual ways of working. The challenge is to make both the structure and its flexibility understandable to users.
